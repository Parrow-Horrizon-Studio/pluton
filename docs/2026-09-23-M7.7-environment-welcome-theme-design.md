# M7.7: Environment presets, the welcome dialog, and the light/dark theme

**Status:** design
**Milestone:** M7.7 (Phase 2.6, Beta readiness)
**Predecessor:** M7.6c Smart-select, selection set operations and vertex selection, shipped v0.12.0
**Target:** v0.13.0
**Issues closed:** #101

## 1. Context

Phase 2.5 closed yesterday at v0.12.0 with M7.6c. Phase 3 (M8 plugin system, M9
Mac support and installers) was next on the roadmap and is deliberately held.
Phase 2.6 opens instead, with a different goal: the application should feel like
SketchUp and its tools should be stable enough to put in front of outside
testers who use SketchUp daily. M7.7 is its first milestone. The milestones that
follow it are issue-driven, working the 66 open issues down.

M7.7 is the look-and-first-run milestone. Three facts about the existing code
shape it, and each was read out of the repository rather than assumed.

**The viewport has exactly one background, and it is a module constant.**
`_BG_COLOR = (0.15, 0.15, 0.18, 1.0)` at `scene_renderer.py:148` is read in three
places: `glClearColor` twice (`:1270`, `:1344`) and, less obviously, as the fill
colour for Hidden Line faces through `resolve_face_pass(..., bg=_BG_COLOR[:3])`
at `:804`. Any design that makes the background configurable has to thread it
through that third site too, and Hidden Line on a white page is a genuinely
different look rather than a recolour.

**Every ink colour in the renderer is tuned for that one dark background.**
`_USER_EDGE_COLOR` is `0.85` light grey and `_GRID_CENTERLINE_COLOR` is `0.60`.
On a white background the model's own edges are effectively invisible. This is the milestone's principal failure mode,
not a polish detail, and section 2.3 exists because of it.

**The dark theme is already built, and unwired.** `pluton/ui/icons/__init__.py`
recolours each monochrome SVG from the palette's text colour at load time, and
caches on `(stem, colour)`. `clear_icon_cache()` exists at `:83` with the
docstring "Used when the palette changes and by tests", and has zero callers.
The gap #101 reports is the refresh, not the capability. That is why light/dark
costs one task here rather than a milestone of its own.

Two further pieces of existing structure are used rather than invented.
`render_style_to_dict` / `render_style_from_dict` in `document_codec.py` already
demonstrate the tolerant persistence pattern this milestone needs:
`from_dict` takes `dict | None` and defaults every key, which is how a schema-5
file still loads. And `Units` is a frozen dataclass held by `DocumentSettings`
and replaced wholesale by `set_units()`, which is exactly the shape the
environment wants.

The application is **Z-up**: the grid lies on XY at z=0
(`_build_grid_vertex_array`, `scene_renderer.py:1022`), so "ground" means
`z < 0` and the horizon is `z == 0`.

## 2. Scope

### 2.1 The Environment value

A new frozen dataclass in `pluton/viewport/environment.py`, Qt-free and GL-free:

```python
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Environment:
    background: tuple[float, float, float]
    sky_enabled: bool
    sky_color: tuple[float, float, float]
    ground_enabled: bool
    ground_color: tuple[float, float, float]
    ground_opacity: float
    edge_color: tuple[float, float, float]
    grid_color: tuple[float, float, float]
    grid_centerline_color: tuple[float, float, float]
```

It lives on `DocumentSettings` beside `Units`, reached as `doc.environment` and
replaced wholesale by `doc.set_environment(env)`. It is **not** a field of
`RenderStyle` (D3) and is **not** captured by a Scene (D4).

The last three fields are the ink, and they are part of the environment rather
than separate settings for one reason: a preset that changes the background
without changing what is drawn on top of it is not a complete preset. Keeping
them together makes every combination readable from one table and lets one test
prove every combination is legible (section 4).

Three presets, and these are the exact values:

| Field | `SKY_AND_GROUND` | `PLAIN_WHITE` | `STUDIO` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `background` | `(1.00, 1.00, 1.00)` | `(1.00, 1.00, 1.00)` | `(0.15, 0.15, 0.18)` |
| `sky_enabled` | `True` | `False` | `False` |
| `sky_color` | `(0.53, 0.71, 0.87)` | `(1.00, 1.00, 1.00)` | `(0.15, 0.15, 0.18)` |
| `ground_enabled` | `True` | `False` | `False` |
| `ground_color` | `(0.72, 0.66, 0.56)` | `(1.00, 1.00, 1.00)` | `(0.15, 0.15, 0.18)` |
| `ground_opacity` | `1.0` | `1.0` | `1.0` |
| `edge_color` | `(0.15, 0.15, 0.15)` | `(0.10, 0.10, 0.10)` | `(0.85, 0.85, 0.85)` |
| `grid_color` | `(0.45, 0.45, 0.45)` | `(0.75, 0.75, 0.75)` | `(0.40, 0.40, 0.40)` |
| `grid_centerline_color` | `(0.30, 0.30, 0.30)` | `(0.55, 0.55, 0.55)` | `(0.60, 0.60, 0.60)` |

`sky_color` and `ground_color` are set to the background in the two presets that
disable them. They are unread in that state, but a preset with a meaningless
value in a live field invites a reader to wonder whether it leaks.

**`STUDIO` reproduces the pre-M7.7 renderer exactly.** Its `background`,
`edge_color`, `grid_color` and `grid_centerline_color` are the current
`_BG_COLOR`, `_USER_EDGE_COLOR`, `_GRID_COLOR` and `_GRID_CENTERLINE_COLOR`
values, unchanged. That is an asserted property, not a coincidence (section 4),
and it is what makes this milestone's renderer refactor provably a no-op for
anyone who keeps the old look.

The user switches environment after startup through **View > Environment**, a
three-action radio group (`view_env_sky_ground`, `view_env_plain_white`,
`view_env_studio`) built the same way the existing units radio group is.
Without that menu the only way to change environment would be the welcome
dialog, which would make the feature nearly unreachable.

### 2.2 The sky and ground pass

A sixth shader pair, `shaders/environment.vert` and `shaders/environment.frag`,
drawing one fullscreen quad immediately after `glClear` and before the grid,
with `GL_DEPTH_TEST` disabled and depth writes off.

The vertex shader emits a full-viewport quad in clip space and passes the NDC
position through. The fragment shader reconstructs a world-space view ray:
unproject `(ndc.x, ndc.y, 1.0)` through `u_inv_view_proj`, divide by `w`,
subtract `u_camera_pos`, and normalise. Then:

- `ray.z > 0` and `u_sky_enabled` gives `u_sky_color`
- `ray.z < 0` and `u_ground_enabled` gives `mix(u_background, u_ground_color, u_ground_opacity)`
- otherwise `u_background`

Because the split is on the ray's Z component rather than on a screen-space
line, the horizon is correct under perspective, sits where the ground plane
actually vanishes, and rolls correctly as the camera orbits. No horizon
geometry is drawn.

Uniforms: `u_inv_view_proj`, `u_camera_pos`, `u_background`, `u_sky_color`,
`u_ground_color`, `u_sky_enabled`, `u_ground_enabled`, `u_ground_opacity`.

`Plain White` needs no special case in the shader: both halves are disabled and
every fragment falls through to `u_background`.

**Background stays a field distinct from sky.** SketchUp's Styles panel carries
Background as its own swatch alongside Sky and Ground, and hidden-line faces
fill with Background. So `Environment.background` drives `glClearColor` and the
`bg=` argument at `scene_renderer.py:804`, while the sky and ground pass draws
over the cleared buffer.

### 2.3 Ink colours and legibility

`_USER_EDGE_COLOR`, `_GRID_COLOR` and `_GRID_CENTERLINE_COLOR` stop being
module constants read at buffer-build time and become values read from
`doc.environment`.

**`MONO_COLOR` deliberately stays a constant.** It was per-environment in an
earlier draft of this design and was removed during spec review, because the
contrast floor it implied was unsatisfiable: with sky and ground enabled the
three backdrops sit at luminance 1.00, 0.683 and 0.666, so no single mono value
clears a 0.15 margin from all of them without going dark, and a dark Monochrome
is not the white-model look the mode exists for. The right conclusion is that
Monochrome faces are lit geometry drawn with the model's edges around them, so
their legibility comes from edge contrast rather than face-to-background
contrast, and `(0.72, 0.72, 0.74)` reads correctly against all three
backgrounds. Keeping it constant also avoids adding a parameter to
`resolve_face_pass`, which reads it directly at `render_style.py:110` and whose
signature change would break the two test files that import it
(`tests/test_render_style.py`, `tests/test_tag_color.py`).

This is not a cosmetic change to the renderer's structure, and the one
consequence worth naming before an implementer discovers it is the vertex data.

**The grid and axes VBOs bake their colours into interleaved vertex data** at
`_init_grid_buffers()` time (`scene_renderer.py:1612`), so a grid colour that
varies per environment either rebuilds that VBO on every environment change or
moves the colour to a uniform. **Move it to a uniform**: the grid is drawn by
`_draw_lines` through the existing line shader, a single colour multiplier costs
one uniform, and rebuilding a VBO on a menu toggle is the kind of thing that
works in a test and stutters in the hand. User geometry edges are per-vertex
coloured the same way and take the same treatment.

The status bar is affected too, and it is not chrome. It is an overlay painted
over the viewport, styled `rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.5)` with `#dddddd` text
(`status_bar.py:19`). Over a white background that resolves to roughly mid-grey
behind light grey text, about 2.3:1, which is unreadable. The chip alpha rises
to `0.72`, which lands near 6.6:1 and works over white, dark and sky alike.
The chip is deliberately **not** coupled to `doc.environment`: one value that
works everywhere beats a second dependency between the status bar and document
state.

### 2.4 Templates

A new pure module, `pluton/templates.py`:

```python
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Template:
    key: str
    name: str
    description: str
    units: Units
    environment: Environment
```

| key | name | units | environment |
|---|---|---|---|
| `architectural` | Architectural | metric, `mm`, precision 0, denom 16 | `SKY_AND_GROUND` |
| `woodworking` | Woodworking | imperial, denom 16, `m`, precision 3 | `SKY_AND_GROUND` |
| `documentation` | Documentation | metric, `m`, precision 3, denom 16 | `PLAIN_WHITE` |
| `studio` | Studio | metric, `m`, precision 3, denom 16 | `STUDIO` |

`DEFAULT_TEMPLATE_KEY = "architectural"`.

Templates differ in **units configuration**, not only in environment, which is
what stops the concept from being a second name for the preset table. `Units`
already carries `metric_precision` and `imperial_denominator`, and those are
exactly what SketchUp's templates vary: its Architectural templates round to
whole millimetres, its Woodworking templates work in sixteenths.

The white preset is called **Documentation**, not "Plan View". Templates do not
set the camera (D6), and a template named Plan View would promise a top-down
view it never applies.

Applying a template is two calls in a fixed order:
`doc.set_units(template.units)` then `doc.set_environment(template.environment)`.

### 2.5 The welcome dialog

`pluton/ui/welcome_dialog.py`. The first QDialog in the application, so it sets
the convention for every dialog after it.

Contents: a template grid showing each template's name and description; a
**units selector** with four entries (Millimetres, Centimetres, Metres, Inches)
initialised from the selected template; an **Open...** button; and a
**Don't show this on startup** checkbox.

Units are a separate axis from the template on purpose. SketchUp welds them
together, which is why its list reads "Architectural Millimeters, Architectural
Inches, Simple Meters, Simple Feet" and multiplies out the same few
environments. Two axes give the same coverage from four templates and one
dropdown, and the template still supplies the default so a tester who ignores
the dropdown gets a sensible unit.

On accept, in this order:

1. `doc.set_units(template.units)`
2. If the selector's unit differs from the template's, override it through
   `doc.set_metric(unit)` for the three metric entries or
   `doc.set_imperial(template.units.imperial_denominator)` for Inches. Both
   setters preserve the fields they do not name, so the template's precision
   and denominator survive the override.
3. `doc.set_environment(template.environment)`
4. Write the chosen template key to `welcome/default_template`.

**Startup sequence.** `app.py` builds and shows `MainWindow` exactly as it does
today, then execs the dialog modally on top of it if
`welcome/show_on_startup` is true. The template applies to the already-live
document. There is deliberately no second "no document yet" code path: one way
a document gets configured, and the dialog drives it.

**Dismissal is non-trapping.** The checkbox writes
`welcome/show_on_startup = False`, and the dialog stays reachable at
**Help > Welcome to Pluton**. Escape or the window close button change nothing
and leave the default template's state intact, so there is no half-configured
document.

**Open... does not reimplement opening.** The dialog reports its outcome and
`MainWindow` routes it into the existing open-file flow. Two open paths drift.

**File > New applies the default template silently**, with no dialog, reading
`welcome/default_template`. That makes the welcome dialog's choice sticky, which
is what a user expects, rather than resetting to a hardcoded favourite.

**The dialog convention this establishes:** `exec()` is never called from
application logic. The dialog exposes its selection as a property, `app.py` is
the only caller that execs it, and tests construct it, drive the widgets and
read the result. Everything interesting is pure and tested without a dialog.

### 2.6 Theme and preferences

`pluton/ui/preferences.py`, holding per-user preference access with the same
discipline `window_state.py` uses: every function takes the `QSettings` object
as a parameter, so tests inject a temporary `IniFormat` store and the suite
touches no real user state. Reads never raise; a corrupt or absent value
returns the default.

| Key | Type | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `appearance/theme` | `"light"` or `"dark"` | `"light"` |
| `welcome/show_on_startup` | bool | `True` |
| `welcome/default_template` | template key | `"architectural"` |

It is a separate module from `window_state.py` rather than an addition to it.
That module is specifically geometry and toolbar layout and carries its own
`WINDOW_STATE_VERSION` which Qt validates and rejects on mismatch; theme and
welcome suppression have no business bumping it.

`pluton/ui/theme.py` holds a two-valued `ThemeChoice` enum and
`apply_theme(app, choice)`, which calls
`app.styleHints().setColorScheme(Qt.ColorScheme.Light | Dark)`. Qt is 6.11, so
this is supported directly.

The control is a two-action radio group in the **View** menu, built like the
existing units group. No Preferences dialog in this milestone (D8).

**The #101 fix.** `MainWindow` handles `QEvent.Type.PaletteChange`, calls
`icons.clear_icon_cache()`, and rebuilds icons from **both** sources: the action
icons reachable from the `ACTIONS` registry, and the non-action icons declared
in `panel_icons.py` (the panel tab strip and the Outliner rows). Rebuilding only
the first set is the obvious way to half-fix this, which is why both are named
here and both are asserted in section 4.

## 4. Testing

The fragment shader's horizon arithmetic runs on the GPU and there is nothing on
the Python side to unit-test. That is the reason section 7 treats the manual
visual pass as a gate rather than a nicety, and it is the honest limit of this
milestone's automated coverage.

Everything else is pure and gets real tests. The named ones that carry weight:

- **`test_studio_preset_matches_the_pre_m77_renderer_constants`** asserts the
  four `STUDIO` fields equal the literal values the renderer used before this
  milestone. This is the regression gate on the whole ink refactor.
- **`test_every_preset_ink_clears_the_contrast_floor`** computes relative
  luminance as `0.2126*r + 0.7152*g + 0.0722*b` and requires, for every preset,
  that `edge_color` differs by at least **0.35** from every *enabled* backdrop
  in that preset (its `background`, plus `sky_color` and `ground_color` where
  enabled), and `grid_color` and `grid_centerline_color` by at least **0.12**.
  Checking against enabled backdrops rather than only `background` is
  the point: with sky and ground on, `background` is not what the user sees
  behind an edge. This test is what stops a future preset shipping invisible
  edges.
- **`test_hidden_line_on_a_white_environment_fills_faces_white`** drives
  `resolve_face_pass`, which is already pure and already tested, with a white
  environment. It discriminates the `scene_renderer.py:804` site specifically:
  a change that reaches `glClearColor` but not Hidden Line passes every other
  test and fails this one.
- **`test_a_schema_8_document_loads_with_the_studio_environment`** builds a
  document dict with no `environment` key and asserts `STUDIO` comes back, not
  the `DocumentSettings` default. The two differ on purpose (D5).
- **`test_environment_round_trips_through_the_codec`** for all ten fields.
- **`test_every_template_environment_is_one_of_the_presets`** and
  **`test_every_template_units_round_trip`**.
- **`test_the_units_override_keeps_the_template_precision`** drives the
  section 2.5 accept sequence with a unit that differs from the template's and
  asserts `metric_precision` and `imperial_denominator` survived.
- **`test_ticking_do_not_show_writes_the_preference`** and
  **`test_a_missing_preference_returns_its_default`**, against an injected
  `IniFormat` store.
- **`test_a_palette_change_rebuilds_the_non_action_icons`**. Named for the half
  that gets forgotten. A companion test covers the action icons.

## 5. Decisions

**D1. Screen-space sky and ground, not a world-space ground quad.** A large
quad on XY at z=0 needs no new shader, but a finite quad has a visible edge, so
at a shallow angle the ground *ends* instead of reaching a horizon. That is
precisely the effect the milestone exists to produce, so the cheaper option
trades away the feature to save a shader pair. A CPU-computed screen-space
gradient was also rejected: its horizon is a straight line in screen space and
lies under perspective tilt and camera roll.

**D2. Ink colours belong to the environment.** The alternative was deriving ink
from background luminance. Rejected: a heuristic produces confidently wrong
output for a mid-grey background, and it makes "why is this edge that colour"
unanswerable from the data. A SketchUp Style carries edge colour and background
together for the same reason.

**D3. Environment lives on `DocumentSettings`, not on `RenderStyle`.**
`RenderStyle` is mutable, is flattened into `SavedView`, and is passed as its
own argument through `save_document`. `Units` is frozen, held by
`DocumentSettings`, and replaced wholesale by a setter, which is exactly the
shape a preset-driven value wants. `doc` is already threaded to
`save_document`.

**D4. A Scene does not capture the environment.** `SavedView` captures render
style field by field (`capture.py:23-25`), so capturing the environment would
add ten flattened fields and churn the scene schema. SketchUp itself lets a
scene leave Style unchecked, so "this scene does not carry the look" is a
legitimate SketchUp state. It stays additive later, with the same defaulting
pattern. Recorded explicitly because it is the decision most likely to be
second-guessed in review, and because an unstated answer here gets decided by
accident.

**D5. `DocumentSettings` defaults to `SKY_AND_GROUND`; the codec defaults to
`STUDIO`.** These differ deliberately. A fresh document is a modelling document
and should open in the modelling environment. A file written before this
milestone was authored against the dark background and should reopen looking
the way its author left it. Unifying them either makes every old file change
appearance on load or makes every new document dark.
`environment_from_dict` also falls back to the `STUDIO` value **per key**, so a
partially written dict still yields a valid `Environment`, matching
`render_style_from_dict`.

**D6. Templates do not set the camera.** It keeps `templates.py` free of a
dependency on the camera module, and it is why the white template is called
Documentation rather than Plan View.

**D7. Light and Dark only, no System.** SketchUp is light on every OS
regardless of system setting, so "light unless asked otherwise" is the faithful
default. A third state triples what the palette-refresh path must be tested
against for no visible gain, and stays trivially addable later since
`Qt.ColorScheme.Unknown` is follow-the-system.

**D8. No Preferences dialog.** The theme and environment controls go in the
View menu as radio groups, matching the existing units group. A Preferences
dialog is where these eventually belong, but building one here would double the
milestone's UI surface to relocate three settings.

**D9. Presets only, no colour pickers.** The user picks among three
environments; there is no arbitrary background, sky or ground colour selection.
SketchUp does allow it, so this is a real and visible gap against SketchUp, and
it is the most likely thing a SketchUp-native tester asks for first. It is
deferred because arbitrary colours break the contrast-floor guarantee in
section 4: the moment a user can set their own background, the invisible-edge
failure mode returns with nothing standing in front of it. Doing it properly
means deriving or prompting for ink alongside it, which is its own design.

**D10. The status bar chip is not environment-coupled.** One alpha value that
reads over white, dark and sky beats a second dependency from the status bar to
document state.

## 6. Out of scope

- **Arbitrary environment colours** (D9). Filed as a follow-up.
- **Sky gradient.** SketchUp's default sky fades toward the horizon. Flat sky
  gives the crisply defined horizon this milestone was asked for; a gradient is
  one extra colour field and one `mix` in the fragment shader later.
- **"Show ground from below".** Orbiting beneath z=0 will show the ground half
  filling the upper screen. Because the pass runs with depth off and before
  geometry, the model still draws on top, so this is cosmetically odd rather
  than blinding. Re-checked during the visual pass; a follow-up issue if it
  looks wrong.
- **Per-scene environment capture** (D4).
- **A Preferences dialog** (D8).
- **Fog.**
- **Recent files and the welcome screen's recent column.** That is #76, its own
  feature: a persisted MRU list, missing-file handling and a File submenu. The
  dialog's layout leaves room for the column.
- **Launch-with-file and OS file association.** Also #76, and the association
  half belongs with M9's installers.

## 7. Risks

**The shader is the one part no test covers, so the visual pass is a gate.**
The standing project rule that renderer work gets a manual render-and-look pass
has caught what tests missed in three consecutive renderer milestones. The
checklist, run at the halt point after task 6 and again at branch end:

1. Each of the three environments at a normal iso camera.
2. Model edge legibility on white.
3. Hidden Line and Monochrome on white.
4. Status bar contrast over white.
5. The horizon while orbiting through level, tilted and below-ground.
6. Both UI themes, including a mid-session switch.
7. The welcome dialog at the default window size.

**Schema 9 splits testers across versions.** `load_document` rejects any file
whose `schema_version` exceeds the running build's, by design. A tester on
v0.12.0 who receives a file saved by v0.13.0 gets `PlutonVersionError`. Nothing
here is wrong, but with a tester group arriving it will happen, so the release
note has to say it plainly.

**Hidden Line on white is a look change, not a bug.** White faces with dark
edges is the correct hidden-line drawing and better than what exists, but
anyone used to the dark viewport will read it as a regression on first sight.

**The grid colour uniform touches a shared path.** Moving grid and edge colour
out of interleaved VBO data and into uniforms changes `_draw_lines` for every
caller, not only the grid. The alternative, rebuilding the VBO when the
environment changes, is worse (section 2.3), but the uniform's blast radius is
wider than this milestone's own scope and wants care in review.

**The icon rebuild has two sources and one obvious half-fix.** Section 2.6 and
the test in section 4 both name `panel_icons.py` explicitly for that reason.

## 8. Sequencing

Nine tasks, one halt point.

1. `Environment`, the three presets, the `STUDIO`-matches-the-old-constants
   test and the contrast-floor test. Pure, no renderer change.
2. Codec: `environment_to_dict` / `from_dict`, `SCHEMA_VERSION` 8 to 9, the
   schema-8 backward-compatibility test.
3. `DocumentSettings.environment`; background threaded to `glClearColor` **and**
   to Hidden Line's fill.
4. The environment shader pair and the draw pass.
5. Ink from the environment: edges, grid and centreline; grid colour to a
   uniform.
6. Status bar chip legibility. **Halt here for the visual pass.**
7. `templates.py` and `preferences.py`. Pure, both fully tested.
8. Welcome dialog, startup wiring, Help > Welcome to Pluton, File > New default
   template.
9. Theme: `apply_theme`, the View menu group, `PaletteChange` refresh across
   both icon sources. Closes #101.

The halt lands where the viewport is visually complete and the legibility
problems are visible, before dialog work piles on top. Same shape as M7.6c's
halt after task 5.
