# Pluton v0.13.0

The viewport gets a look you can choose, the application gets a first-run
window, and the interface gets a light and a dark theme.

This is the first release of Phase 2.6, which exists to get Pluton to the point
where people who use SketchUp every day can try it and tell us what is wrong.
If that is you: thank you, and please read the compatibility note below before
you start, because it is the one thing in this release that can waste your
afternoon.

---

## Read this first: files saved in 0.13.0 cannot be opened by 0.12.0

The `.pluton` format moved to `schema_version` 9 so that a model can remember
its own environment. Older builds refuse a newer file rather than guessing at
it, so they will report an error rather than opening it with the wrong
appearance.

**If you are sharing models with anyone, everyone needs to be on 0.13.0.**

Going the other way is fine. Files written by 0.12.0 and earlier open normally,
and they keep the dark viewport they were drawn against. That is deliberate: a
model should look the way its author left it, not the way the current default
happens to be.

## What is new

### Five viewport environments

`View` now offers **Sky and Ground**, **Neutral Grey**, **Landscape**, **Plain
White** and **Studio**. Sky and ground meet at a real horizon that stays correct
as you orbit and tilt, because it is derived from where the ground plane
actually vanishes rather than drawn as a line.

The environment is saved **in the file**, so a model you send someone looks the
way you left it.

`Studio` is the dark viewport Pluton had through v0.12, unchanged. If you liked
it, pick it and nothing about the way your models look will move.

Each environment also carries the colours of the things drawn against it: the
model's edges, the grid, and the ground grid's centre lines. That sounds like an
implementation detail and is not. The old edge colour was a light grey chosen
for a dark background, and on a white page it was invisible. Every environment
is now checked, automatically, to keep its ink readable against its own
backdrop.

### A welcome window

Pluton now opens with a window offering six templates. A template pairs an
environment with a units setup, and units are a separate dropdown, so you can
take any template and work in whatever unit you like.

| Template | Environment | Units |
|---|---|---|
| Architectural | Sky and Ground | millimetres, whole units |
| Woodworking | Sky and Ground | inches, to the nearest sixteenth |
| Presentation | Neutral Grey | metres |
| Site | Landscape | metres |
| Documentation | Plain White | metres |
| Studio | Studio | metres |

You can turn the window off with the checkbox, and get it back from
**Help > Welcome to Pluton**. `File > New` quietly uses whichever template you
picked last.

### Light and dark themes

`View > Light Theme` and `View > Dark Theme`. Light is the default. Icons now
follow the theme when you switch, which they did not before.

## Four things that will look different, and are meant to

1. **`File > New` now gives you millimetres, not metres.** The default template
   is Architectural. Change it by picking a different template in the welcome
   window; your choice sticks.
2. **The interface is light unless you choose dark.** It used to follow your
   operating system. SketchUp is light on every platform and Pluton now matches
   that, with dark one menu click away.
3. **A new document opens in Sky and Ground, but an old file reopens dark.**
   Those are different on purpose, and it is the honest behaviour rather than an
   inconsistency: new work gets the current default, existing work keeps what it
   was drawn against.
4. **Hidden Line looks different on a light background.** White faces with dark
   edges is what hidden-line drawing is supposed to look like. It was previously
   dark-on-dark because there was only ever a dark background.

## Known gaps

Things you will probably notice, already recorded so you do not need to report
them:

- **You cannot pick your own environment colours**, only the five presets. This
  is the gap most likely to bother a SketchUp user, since SketchUp lets you set
  background, sky and ground freely. It is deferred rather than forgotten: an
  arbitrary background removes the guarantee that keeps edges readable, so doing
  it properly means solving the ink alongside it.
- **A checked toolbar icon can be hard to read against its own highlight**
  ([#128](https://github.com/Parrow-Horrizon-Studio/pluton/issues/128)). The
  obvious fix was measured and does not work; the issue carries the data.
- **The grid cannot be hidden, and its size and spacing are fixed**
  ([#127](https://github.com/Parrow-Horrizon-Studio/pluton/issues/127)).
- **Vertex selection mode shows no marker under the cursor**
  ([#125](https://github.com/Parrow-Horrizon-Studio/pluton/issues/125)).

## Reporting a problem

Open an issue at
<https://github.com/Parrow-Horrizon-Studio/pluton/issues>. Useful things to
include: the version from the console line Pluton prints at startup, which
environment and theme you were in, and what you expected instead. A screenshot
beats a description for anything about how it looks.

If Pluton refuses to open a file, check the version it was saved with before
anything else.
