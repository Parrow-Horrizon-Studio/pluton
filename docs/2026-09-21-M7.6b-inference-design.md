# M7.6b: Inference, construction guides, and on-canvas measurement

**Status:** design
**Milestone:** M7.6b (Phase 2.5, Interaction parity)
**Predecessor:** M7.6a Face split, shipped v0.10.0
**Target:** v0.11.0
**Issues closed:** #31 (remaining items), #108

## 1. Context

M7.6 was split into three sub-milestones during its design. M7.6a delivered the
kernel half, `HalfEdgeMesh::split_face` and the tools that drive it. M7.6b is the
interaction half: the inference machinery that makes drawing precise, the
construction guides that give inference something deliberate to aim at, and the
on-canvas readout that tells you what you are about to commit. M7.6c (smart-select)
follows.

Three findings during this design changed the milestone's shape, and all three are
pre-existing defects rather than new work. Each was measured, not inferred.

**Axis lock is unreachable over a face.** `SnapEngine._face_candidate` reports
`screen_dist=0.0` unconditionally, so an `ON_FACE` candidate is always within
`PIXEL_TOLERANCE` while the cursor ray hits a face, and `_PRECEDENCE` ranks
`ON_FACE` above `AXIS_LOCK`. Measured on a 10x10 quad with the anchor at
`(2, 2, 0)`: at five sampled pixels lying exactly on the red axis through the
anchor and over the face, the snap returned `ON_FACE` at every one. Drawing on a
face is the ordinary case, so the axis inference pluton already shipped is largely
invisible in practice.

**The drawing plane is wrong at a face's own corner.** `shape_support.resolve_drawing_plane`
keys off `snap.kind == SnapKind.ON_FACE`, and `SnapResult.face_id` is populated only
by the `ON_FACE` candidate. Measured on a vertical wall in the `x = 0` plane: at the
wall's corner the snap is `ENDPOINT` with `face_id=None` and the resolved plane
normal is `[0, 0, 1]`; at its midpoint, `MIDPOINT` with `face_id=None` and again
`[0, 0, 1]`; only in the wall's interior does it resolve to the wall's own
`[1, 0, 0]`. Starting a rectangle or a circle at a wall's corner therefore drops it
onto a horizontal plane. This is independent of M7.6b, and demoting `ON_FACE` would
turn it from an edge case into the normal case, so it is sequenced first.

**The Measurements box is near-dead UI.** `StatusBar.set_status` is its only setter
and `main_window.py:562` is its only caller, feeding the typed VCB buffer. Seventeen
tools already override `status_text`, but that lands in the prompt area as prose.
The numeric field a user looks at while drawing is blank unless they type.

The milestone therefore reads: make the inference already present reachable, then
make it richer.

## 2. Scope

### 2.1 Reachability fixes

Three changes, sequenced before anything new.

1. `SnapResult.face_id` is populated whenever the cursor ray hits a face, whichever
   candidate kind wins. `resolve_drawing_plane` keys off `snap.face_id is not None`
   rather than `snap.kind == SnapKind.ON_FACE`.
2. `ON_FACE` moves below the directional inferences in `_PRECEDENCE`.

`_face_candidate`'s `screen_dist=0.0` stays. It looks like a shortcut but it is
exact: the candidate's position is the ray-face intersection, so it reprojects onto
the cursor by construction. Measured across 84 sampled face hits, the worst
reprojection error was 1.5e-4 pixels. The defect is entirely in the precedence
table, not in the distance.

Together these make axis lock, Parallel, and Perpendicular reachable while drawing
on a face, without losing the face plane that shape tools depend on.

### 2.2 New inference kinds

All are pure candidate generators in the new `snap_candidates.py`.

- **`PARALLEL`** against an acquired edge. Well defined in 3D as the line through
  the gesture anchor along the edge's direction. Magenta.
- **`PERPENDICULAR`** against an acquired edge. Not well defined in 3D on its own,
  so it resolves inside the gesture's drawing plane: direction
  `normalize(cross(n, d))` for plane normal `n` and edge direction `d`. The
  candidate is skipped when `abs(dot(n, d)) > 1.0 - _PLANE_PARALLEL_EPS` with
  `_PLANE_PARALLEL_EPS = 1e-4`, where the cross product degenerates. Magenta.
- **`FROM_POINT`** from an acquired vertex, which radiates the three axis lines.
  Drawn dotted in the axis colour. This is what lets a user align to geometry the
  line never touches.
- **`ON_GUIDE`** and **`GUIDE_POINT`**, once guides exist (2.4).
- Guide-versus-edge and guide-versus-guide crossings feed the existing
  `INTERSECTION` kind, because that is what they are.

Each behaves like `AXIS_LOCK` does today: find the point on the infinite line
through the anchor nearest the cursor ray, project it, and test pixel tolerance.

Marker colours join `MARKER_COLOR_BY_KIND`. `FROM_POINT` has no entry of its own:
it takes the colour of the axis it rides, from the existing axis palette.

| Kind | RGB |
|---|---|
| `PARALLEL` | `(0.82, 0.23, 0.82)` |
| `PERPENDICULAR` | `(0.82, 0.23, 0.82)` |
| `INTERSECTION` | `(0.10, 0.10, 0.12)`, changed from magenta by D14 |
| `GUIDE_POINT` | `(0.45, 0.45, 0.52)` |
| `ON_GUIDE` | `(0.45, 0.45, 0.52)` |

### 2.3 Acquisition and locking

Acquisition is hover-dwell. Resting the cursor on a vertex or an edge for
`DWELL_MS = 250` acquires it; moving off it by more than `PIXEL_TOLERANCE` releases
it. An acquired vertex yields `FROM_POINT` candidates, an acquired edge yields
`PARALLEL` and `PERPENDICULAR`. At most one reference is acquired at a time; a new
acquisition replaces the old.

Locking pins the inference regardless of cursor position:

| Key | Lock |
|---|---|
| `Shift` (hold) | whatever inference is currently showing |
| Up arrow | blue axis (Z) |
| Left arrow | green axis (Y) |
| Right arrow | red axis (X) |
| Down arrow | the acquired edge direction |

An arrow-key lock toggles: pressing the same arrow again releases it. `Escape`
releases any lock, and a lock also releases when the gesture ends. An active lock
bypasses precedence entirely and wins outright; the cursor then chooses only the
distance along the locked line.

Both acquisition and locking live in `InferenceState`, which takes an injected
`now()` so dwell is a value in tests rather than a timer.

### 2.4 Construction guides

Guides ride `Definition.annotations`. That rail already gives them context-local
placement, persistence, hit-testing, selection, erasing, and undo:
`Selection` tracks annotation ids; `EraseTool` already deletes annotations through
`pick_annotation` and `DeleteAnnotationsCommand`; `CreateAnnotationCommand` and
`DeleteAnnotationsCommand` exist with working undo; `annotation_to_dict` and
`annotation_from_dict` are kind-discriminated.

Two dataclasses in `model/annotation.py`, following `Dimension` and `Label`
exactly, storing context-local coordinates as plain 3-tuples:

```python
@dataclass
class Guide:
    """An infinite construction line: a point on it and a unit direction."""
    id: int
    origin: Point
    direction: Point
    kind: str = "guide"


@dataclass
class GuidePoint:
    """A construction point with no geometry attached."""
    id: int
    position: Point
    kind: str = "guide_point"
```

`Guide.__post_init__` normalises `direction` and raises `ValueError` on a
zero-length one, so no downstream consumer has to guard.

**Layout.** `_plan_guide` and `_plan_guide_point` join `plan_annotation`'s
dispatch. A guide is infinite, so its layout clips the 3D line against the camera
near plane *before* projecting, then clips the resulting 2D segment to the viewport
rectangle. Projecting two far-apart sample points is wrong: it breaks when the
guide runs near-parallel to the view direction and it produces garbage when part of
the line is behind the camera. Guides draw as thin dashed lines; guide points draw
as a small dashed cross.

**Creation.** `TapeMeasureTool` creates guides by default, matching SketchUp, and
`Ctrl` returns it to today's measure-only behaviour.

Clicking an edge and dragging produces a guide parallel to that edge. The offset is
measured perpendicular to the edge *within the plane of the face the edge bounds*,
and the drag's component along any other direction is discarded, so the guide stays
coplanar with the surface the user is working on. A free edge bounding no live face
falls back to the gesture's drawing plane; an edge bounding two faces uses the one
under the cursor at click time.

Clicking a point and dragging along a direction, optionally typing a distance into
the VCB, produces a guide point at that distance along the resolved direction.

Erasing works with no new code: a guide's draw plan produces screen-space segments,
so `pick_annotation`'s existing `_segment_box` hit-test covers it, and the Eraser's
current annotation branch deletes it.

**UI.** `View > Guides` as a checkable action alongside `view_xray`, hiding all
guides without deleting them. `Edit > Delete Guides` removes every guide in the
active context through `DeleteAnnotationsCommand`, so it undoes as one step.

**Schema.** `SCHEMA_VERSION` goes 7 to 8. `annotation_from_dict` raises
`PlutonFormatError` on an unknown kind, so the bump is the honest signal to an older
build that it cannot read the file.

### 2.5 On-canvas measurement

A new `Tool.measurement_text` property, defaulting to `None`, returning a numeric
value only and deliberately distinct from the prose `status_text`. Lengths go
through the existing `pluton.units.format_length` so the readout honours the
document's units exactly as dimensions already do; angles format as `12 deg`,
matching the ASCII convention `rotate_tool.py:266` already uses, and
two-axis values join with ` x ` (a rectangle at 2.5 by 4 metres reads
`2.5 m x 4 m`). It drives two sinks:

1. The status bar's Measurements box, whenever the VCB is idle. This fills a field
   that is empty today.
2. An on-canvas readout beside the cursor, laid out by `draw_plan.plan_cursor_readout`
   and painted by the same `_paint_annotations` path.

Typing into the VCB takes over both, showing the buffer and caret where the live
value was. The readout is laid out in `draw_plan`, so the recording-stub painter the
annotation tests already use covers it headless.

Tools implementing `measurement_text` in this milestone: Line, Rectangle, Circle,
Arc, Polygon, Push/Pull, Move, Rotate, Scale, Offset, Tape Measure. Each is a small
property reusing the value it already computes.

### 2.6 Issue #31, remaining items

Two of #31's five items shipped in M7.6a and are verified closed against the tree:
`SplitEdgeCommand`'s asserts all carry messages (`scene_commands.py:446`, `:449`,
`:466`, `:467`), and the Line tool's double-click finish gesture exists
(`line_tool.py:198`). The three live items:

- `SnapEngine._select` tiebreaks on `(precedence, screen_dist, depth)` rather than
  `(precedence, depth)`, so a candidate under the cursor cannot lose to one several
  pixels away but nearer the camera. This bites within a single kind, where two
  endpoints or two midpoints compete and only depth currently separates them.
- `_axis_candidates` skips an axis when the cursor ray is within
  `_COLLINEAR_DEG = 1.0` of collinear with it, where the two-line closest-point
  solve is ill-conditioned. This also suppresses the rare collinear-edge spurious
  intersection.
- A GoogleTest for sequential `split_edge` calls on the same face.

### 2.7 Issue #108

- `RectangleTool` converges on `PrimitiveTool`'s local-frame plane resolution,
  replacing the literal world `z = 0` at `rectangle_tool.py:123-130`, `:174`, and
  `:197`.
- `SnapEngine`'s grid snap intersects the active context's local ground plane and
  transforms the result to world, instead of building `[gx, gy, 0.0]` in world space
  regardless of the active transform.
- A test under a rotated *and* translated active context. Rotation is the case where
  a wrong z corrupts local x and y as well, and only translation is exercised today.

## 4. Testing

Everything structural is pure and lands in pytest: candidate generators, precedence
selection, the inference state machine driven by an injected clock, guide layout
including the near-plane clip, codec round-trip in both directions, and readout
text. Each test must *fail* against the specific broken implementation it names, as
in M7.6a. Mutation checks are the evidence, not the claim.

Specific cases worth naming because they are the ones that hide:

- Axis lock wins at a pixel exactly on the axis line *and* over a face. This is the
  regression test for 2.1 and it fails against today's code.
- The drawing plane at a wall's corner and midpoint resolves to the wall's normal.
  Fails against today's code, as measured in section 1.
- A guide running near-parallel to the view direction, and a guide passing behind
  the camera, both lay out without producing off-screen garbage.
- A dwell that has not yet elapsed acquires nothing; one clock tick later it does.
- A v7 file still loads. A v8 file containing both guide kinds round-trips.
- A rotated plus translated active context places a rectangle footprint and a grid
  snap on the context's own floor (#108).

One ctest addition for #31's sequential `split_edge`.

**The manual render-and-look gate is mandatory for this milestone.** Guides and the
readout are both drawn, and that gate has caught what tests missed in three
consecutive renderer milestones. The gate must also be shown to discriminate, by
perturbing the implementation and confirming the render visibly changes.

## 5. Decisions

**D1. A stateful inference layer over a stateless snap engine.** `SnapEngine` keeps
one job and no memory. Acquisition and locking, which are stateful and
time-dependent, live in `inference.py`. Growing `SnapEngine` in place would have
made every existing snap test carry a clock argument and would have given one module
three unrelated concerns.

**D2. `ON_FACE` drops below the directional inferences.** Measured cost of not
doing this: axis lock never wins over a face. The candidate's `screen_dist=0.0` is
left alone, because it is exact rather than a shortcut.

**D3. `face_id` is populated whichever kind wins, and `resolve_drawing_plane` keys
off it.** This is what makes D2 safe. Audited: `push_pull_tool`, `offset_tool`,
`follow_me_tool`, and `rotate_tool` all call `ray_pick_face` themselves and read
`hit.face_id`, not `snap.face_id`, so `resolve_drawing_plane` is the single consumer
affected.

**D4. Perpendicular resolves in the gesture's drawing plane.** Perpendicular to a
line in 3D is a plane, not a direction. Resolving it against the drawing plane is
what SketchUp does and what a user means. The candidate is skipped rather than
guessed when the edge runs along the plane normal.

**D5. Acquisition is hover-dwell with an injected clock.** Dwell is what a
SketchUp user's hands already know; a modifier key would make every acquisition a
deliberate act. Injecting `now()` at the constructor is what keeps it out of the
flaky-test category.

**D6. Arrow-key locks toggle; Shift-lock is held.** Matches SketchUp. A toggle is
the right shape for arrows because a lock often outlives the keypress, and holding
is right for Shift because it is momentary by nature.

**D7. Guides are annotations, not a new entity family.** The rail already carries
context-local placement, persistence, picking, selection, erasing, and undo. A
parallel entity family would duplicate all six.

**D8. Guide lines clip against the near plane in 3D before projection.** Sampling
two far-apart points and projecting them is wrong for near-parallel guides and
produces garbage for anything behind the camera.

**D9. Tape Measure creates guides by default; `Ctrl` reverts to measure-only.**
SketchUp's default. Today's measure-only behaviour becomes the `Ctrl` branch rather
than being lost.

**D10. `measurement_text` is numeric only and distinct from `status_text`.** The
prose prompt segment and the numeric field answer different questions, and the
numeric one needs to be safe to drop into a box beside the cursor.

**D11. Center inference is deferred.** SketchUp reads it off a curve entity that
knows its own centre and radius. Pluton has none: `shape_support.build_closed_face`
takes raw world points and a circle commits as an N-segment ring. Approximating with
a face centroid would answer a different question, reporting points on a rectangle
or an L-shaped face that SketchUp would never offer, and falling outside a concave
face entirely. The curve entity belongs with smart-select, where it also buys
one-click circle selection, after-the-fact segment-count editing, and true arc
tangency.

**D12. Precedence, highest first:**

```
ENDPOINT > INTERSECTION > MIDPOINT > GUIDE_POINT > ON_EDGE > ON_GUIDE
         > PERPENDICULAR > PARALLEL > FROM_POINT > AXIS_LOCK > ON_FACE > GRID
```

Geometry outranks guides at each tier, guides outrank directional inferences, and
`ON_FACE` sits just above the grid fallback. An active lock bypasses the table.

**D13. Schema goes 7 to 8.** Guides are new persisted kinds and
`annotation_from_dict` raises on an unknown kind, so an older build must be told.

**D14. Parallel and Perpendicular take magenta; `INTERSECTION` moves to near-black.**
SketchUp uses magenta for parallel and perpendicular and a dark marker for
intersections. Pluton currently spends magenta on `INTERSECTION`, so the faithful
colours would collide. Changing `INTERSECTION` rather than inventing a second
magenta keeps the two distinguishable and lands on SketchUp's own convention. This
is a deliberate change to shipped behaviour, recorded here rather than made quietly.

## 6. Out of scope

- **Center inference and the curve entity** (D11). Goes with smart-select.
- **The Protractor tool and angled guides.** A third guide-creating gesture on top
  of two new schema kinds is more than this milestone should carry.
- **Tangent-at-vertex inference for the Arc tool.** Needs the curve entity.
- **Smart-select** (M7.6c).
- **`ArcTool` mid-edge snap resolution** (#122). Named here only because it is
  inference-adjacent; it is a separate change to a second interactive tool.

## 7. Risks

**Demoting `ON_FACE` moves behaviour every existing snap test feels.** Mitigated by
sequencing D3 first and by the audit in D3 showing a single affected consumer.
Expect churn in snap test expectations, and treat any test that needs its expected
kind changed as a question rather than a mechanical edit.

**Hover-dwell is the only time-dependent behaviour in the codebase.** If the clock
leaks in anywhere other than `InferenceState`'s constructor, the result is flaky
tests that pass locally and fail in CI. The design admits exactly one injection
point.

**Infinite-line clipping has more edge cases than it looks.** Near-parallel to the
view, entirely behind the camera, passing exactly through the camera position, and
grazing a viewport corner. Each gets a test.

**The precedence table is a behavioural contract with no single owner.** Twelve
kinds is enough that a future addition can silently reorder something. The table
lives in one list with the rank derived from it, and the tests assert specific
pairwise outcomes rather than the list's contents.

**Guides make `.pluton` files unreadable by v0.10.x.** Intended, and the schema bump
is the signal, but it is the first time a pluton file has been forward-incompatible
since M7.5c.

## 8. Sequencing

1. **Reachability.** `face_id` always populated, `resolve_drawing_plane` keyed off
   it, `ON_FACE` precedence demotion, `_select` tiebreak (#31). Lands as one task
   because the pieces are unsafe apart.
2. **Extraction.** Candidate generators move to `snap_candidates.py`, behaviour
   unchanged. A pure refactor with the existing suite as its gate.
3. **`InferenceState`.** Dwell acquisition and locking, with the injected clock.
   Wired at `_snap_for_event`.
4. **Directional inferences.** `PARALLEL`, `PERPENDICULAR`, `FROM_POINT`, and the
   `INTERSECTION` colour change (D14).
5. **Guides, model and persistence.** `Guide`, `GuidePoint`, codec, schema 8.
6. **Guides, layout and render.** `_plan_guide`, `_plan_guide_point`, near-plane
   clipping.
7. **Guides, creation and UI.** Tape Measure gesture, `View > Guides`,
   `Edit > Delete Guides`.
8. **Guide inference.** `ON_GUIDE`, `GUIDE_POINT`, guide crossings.
9. **On-canvas measurement.** `measurement_text`, both sinks, the readout layout.
10. **#108 and #31's remainder.** Rectangle local frame, grid snap under the active
    transform, the rotated-plus-translated test, the `_axis_candidates` collinear
    guard, the sequential `split_edge` GoogleTest.

**Halt point after task 4.** Tasks 1 to 4 are a complete, shippable improvement on
their own: the inference that exists becomes reachable and the directional
inferences arrive, with no schema change. Tasks 5 onward add persisted state. If the
milestone needs to stop early, it stops there.
